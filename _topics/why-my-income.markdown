## The Agency I Had Never Heard Of {#chapter-1}

“Didn't you notice what they did to Shakira?” Lena asks. I’ve just told her Spain has nothing to do with the money my German clients pay me. Now I’m waiting for her to laugh.

We’re in my kitchen in Valencia, trying to book a weekend away. I said it would be good to leave Spain for a few days anyway. Reset the clock. When she asks what clock, I almost laugh myself. The 183 days. How does she not know this?

I reach for my phone. Another freelancer explained it on X: keep moving, don’t stay six months, you’re fine. People in the replies were comparing places to go next. I’d recommended Lisbon, pleased that I finally knew enough to help someone else.

“Maya, it’s more than 183 days across the calendar year. Going away doesn’t start you at zero.” I find the post anyway and push my phone toward her. Perhaps she’s misunderstood. Perhaps there’s something in the replies that makes us both right.

She opens a page headed Agencia Tributaria. I ask who that is, and she tells me it’s the Spanish tax agency. I’ve never heard the name. Now I’m reading its rules at the table where I earn my living, trying to work out whether they already apply to me.

“But my clients are in Germany. They pay my German account.” I hear myself saying it again, more slowly, as though Lena hasn’t understood. She has. She says that doesn’t settle where I’m tax resident.

There’s a website open on my laptop that I stayed up until two finishing. I found that client, negotiated the price, spent weeks getting everything right. When did choosing to work from this kitchen give Spain a claim on what I earned?

I want Lena to show me the sentence that says I’m fine. She says days aren’t the only test, and we need someone to check my situation. She hasn’t said I owe anything. I’m already subtracting an amount I don’t know from money I thought I could use.

After she leaves, I keep reopening the agency’s page. Nobody has contacted me, yet I’m searching old flight confirmations as though someone is standing behind me, waiting for an answer.

I open X to delete my Lisbon reply. Someone beneath it has written, “Booking Spain now. Thanks!” For a moment I envy them. They still feel the way I felt this morning. Then I start typing: “Please check this before you book. I relied on it too.”

Which tax rule did you first hear from another freelancer?
