## 1. The Fire Belonged to Everyone

The hunters return to a circle in which survival, story, ritual, and belonging are still the same thing. Nobody asks what life is for; tomorrow's hunger is answer enough.

## 2. The Grain That Could Be Counted

The first harvest creates a future that can be stored, guarded, measured, and stolen. Purpose begins to separate into roles: the one who plants, the one who records, the one who commands, and the gods who make the arrangement seem permanent.

## 3. A Place Beneath the Gods

In the first cities, a person's meaning comes from position within an order larger than any individual life. The temple, the household, and the ruler turn duty into a map of the universe.

## 4. Bread and Games

Rome feeds the crowd and gives it spectacle, but the arena offers more than distraction. For an afternoon, thousands become one body, certain who deserves glory, who deserves death, and where they belong.

## 5. The Bell, the Field, and Heaven

Time is rung from a tower, work follows the seasons, and suffering is given an eternal horizon. A life may be narrow, but it arrives with an inherited answer to why it must be lived.

## 6. When the Clock Entered the Body

The factory detaches labor from weather and binds it to hours. Work becomes something sold, discipline becomes a virtue, and usefulness begins to look like a wage.

## 7. The Career and the Good Life

The modern worker is offered a new covenant: education, employment, consumption, retirement. Purpose becomes personal achievement, while leisure becomes the reward for having proved one's economic value.

## 8. Everyone in the Arena

The network puts the games in every hand and invites everyone to perform. Attention becomes status, identity becomes continuous publication, and the crowd no longer needs an emperor to keep watching.

## 9. 2026: What If We Are Not Needed?

AI begins to perform the tasks through which millions learned to demonstrate intelligence, ambition, and worth. Beneath the fear of losing income is a quieter terror: if the bread remains and the games never end, what will tell us that our lives matter?
